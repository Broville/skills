# Group Study Methods

All methods below preserve the same order: text before interpretation; interpretation before response; discussion before declaration.

## Inductive Study

1. Observation — What does the passage say?
2. Interpretation — What did it mean in context?
3. Response — How should participants consider responding?

Use for most passages when the group needs a clear, repeatable sequence.

## Manuscript Study

Present the passage with room for marking. Participants identify repetitions, questions, transitions, and structure before consulting secondary material.

Use for passages with visible structure, parallelism, or recurring terms.

## Four-Question Study

Ask: What does it say? What is difficult or challenging? What does it show about God? What response does it invite?

Use for mixed-experience groups or low-preparation settings.

## Discovery Study

Ask what the passage says about God and people, what the group may need to obey or share, and what help participants need.

Use when reproducibility, oral participation, and practical response are central.

## Selection Guide

| Situation | Starting method |
|---|---|
| Mixed experience | Four-question or inductive |
| Strong visual learners or structured passage | Manuscript |
| Reproducible discussion group | Discovery |
| Experienced group studying a dense passage | Inductive plus manuscript |
