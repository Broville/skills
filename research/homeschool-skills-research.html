What is ready

32 document-only packages cover curriculum planning, lesson preparation, assessment, access supports, retention, literacy, mixed-age and outdoor learning, crafts, and five household records workflows. They are staged for review on the feature branch; review and merge remain separate from the intake.

22 packages derive from Gareth Manning’s education library, two from Learning Commons, two from SkillMedev, one from Jellypod, and five are portable adaptations of Brad Busenius’s Charlotte workflows. Licenses and source revisions travel with each package. No student records or family configuration are included.

The source bodies are preserved for teaching skills, apart from packaging references; the governing usage contract makes host-specific outputs, missing-source disclosures and attribution explicit. Charlotte adaptations retain record provenance, deduplication, backups and protection of manual edits, while replacing its family-specific command layer with portable document procedures.

Primary sources and credibility

Institutional co-development, an identified educator’s public work, or a named primary project maintainer are stronger provenance than a marketplace listing. Community/vendor sources are explicitly distinguished from institutional sources. Popularity and a scanner badge are not evidence of teaching effectiveness.

The institutional relationship is confirmed by Learning Commons’ announcement and Anthropic’s repository. Manning’s practitioner background is described in his public CV; this is not an independent validation of every research citation or instructional claim.

Security evidence

32/32 additions: zero findings in the final scanner pass; no unresolved intake concern.

Cisco AI Skill Scanner 2.3.2 ran static, bytecode, pipeline, correlation, behavioral and OSV analyzers. Downloaded source repositories stayed quarantined; candidate scripts and installers were not executed. The final package inventory contains only documents, JSON metadata, CSV templates and license notices.

The unmodified strict scan flagged generated .codex-plugin/plugin.json as hidden data, along with three overlong descriptions. Descriptions were shortened. Each hidden directory was checked to contain only its regenerated catalog JSON with no hooks, apps, MCP servers or payload. The published policy differs from Cisco strict only by recognizing that conventional packaging directory; it leaves content analysis enabled and all other strict settings intact. The final run covered all 41 productivity packages and returned zero findings at every severity.

Upstream flags were retained as evidence and affected packages were not admitted. Examples include access-to-education and the education library’s ai-claim-checker. These are automated risk signals, not claims of malicious authorship or validated exploitable vulnerabilities. A clean result in a selected adaptation does not approve its upstream repository as a whole.

Primary-source and license review, instruction/privacy review, portable-record workflow checks, a symlink/executable inventory, token/private-key patterns and Unicode concealment checks supplement the scanner. The machine-readable intake records candidate findings, revisions, hashes and decisions; the scan policy makes the packaging exception reviewable.

skill-scanner scan-all skills/productivity --recursive --policy research/homeschool-skill-scan-policy.yaml --use-behavioral --use-osv --fail-on-severity info --format json --output <report>

Verification

Repository validation passed for 118 skills with zero warnings; installer adapters, 129 category/individual plugin packages, marketplace validation and generated-metadata checks passed. Package tests now verify that Apache licenses and concise display titles survive regeneration. The installer checks the actual source inventory instead of a stale hardcoded count.

Limits

  • Automated scans are best-effort, not security certification. Upstream severity flags are candidates, not confirmed vulnerabilities.
  • No candidate script, installer, hook, external service or student-data integration was run. Admitted packages contain no executable code or dependency manifests; OSV therefore does not establish a runtime-dependency safety claim.
  • No scanner LLM/cloud judge, VirusTotal, or AI Defense cloud service was used. This agent performed the semantic intake review.
  • The Codex Security workflow shared reference/preflight resources could not be read in this environment; this is a Cisco scan plus local review, not a completed Codex Security scan.
  • No live classroom, legal-compliance, long-term learner-outcome or third-party connector evaluation was performed.
  • The final scan covers 41 productivity packages; the admission decision and content hashes apply only to the 32 additions.
  • Search-engine indexing, inaccessible content, private repositories and newly published skills prevent a literal claim to have exhausted the entire internet.

Coverage and gaps

The intake supports the teaching cycle and private learning records. Curriculum coverage is a document comparison, not proof of teaching or legal compliance. Time records preserve evidence but do not decide which hours a jurisdiction counts.

  • Legal administration: no portable, sufficiently verified jurisdiction-specific homeschool notice/filing skill was admitted. Use the relevant education department and current statutes after the household’s jurisdiction is known.
  • Transcripts and credits: no specialist transcript/GPA/credit-award package passed this intake; do not turn the time ledger into an accredited transcript.
  • Calendar and co-op operations: service connectors exist, but no calendar connection, schedule, enrollment, message or booking was created.
  • Budget and purchasing: no independently supported homeschool-specific expense/ESA reimbursement skill was selected; existing finance skills are not a substitute for local reimbursement rules.
  • Special education: access supports are included; diagnosis, IEP eligibility and clinical assessment are outside these packages.
  • Portfolios and progress reports: lesson records and feedback provide evidence for a later portfolio; no official legal or accreditation submission is generated automatically.

Existing Broville PDF, flashcard, transcript and notebook skills can support appropriate outputs. Official Google Workspace workflows are connector-specific and already available in this host; copying them into a generic package would not create that access.

Search method and stopping point

35 web queries plus one GitHub repository query covered homeschool and adjacent K–12 terminology, lesson/curriculum design, records, attendance, transcripts, legal compliance, calendars, co-ops, accessibility, Montessori, budgets and field trips. Discovery used indexed GitHub pages, skills directories and official publisher sites, then followed promising leads to primary repositories and license files. Ten candidate corpora plus the user-required Ponytail source were downloaded and scanned. Companion/fork/marketplace duplicates were not counted as independent endorsements.

Later targeted searches mostly repeated Charlotte, the education library, institutional K–12 skills and directory listings. That is practical search saturation, not proof that every internet skill has been found. Private, unindexed, inaccessible and future packages remain outside coverage. Source counts include duplicate companion and support entrypoints and are deliberately not presented as unique vetted skills.
